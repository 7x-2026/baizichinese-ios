The Buckwheat Girl

There was a family—both parents had died, leaving behind a thirteen-year-old boy named "Mangsheng."

The parents had worked hard all their lives, and at last they managed to leave Mangsheng a piece of wasteland by the riverbank. Everyone said that the land wouldn’t grow anything. True enough, when the river flooded, the water would soak the land, and when strong winds blew, sand would cover it. By the time Mangsheng turned seventeen, though he was still young, he was already a strong and determined young man. He began to take charge of the land himself. Others said, "Mangsheng, don’t waste your time on this."

Mangsheng replied, "Wait and see." He didn’t argue with them—he just kept working harder. Indeed, nothing in the world is impossible if you put in the effort.

Mangsheng cleared away the sand and dug out the roots of weeds. By the riverbank, he built a high stone wall.

Once the land was prepared, spring had passed. Mangsheng thought: "It’s too late to plant rice or millet. After much thought, I’ll just plant buckwheat instead. Everyone says buckwheat doesn’t require much work."

Cangjie started working in the buckwheat field from the very first day, staying there like a nail driven firmly into place. Every day, he tilled the soil, applied manure, and did all the hard work—nothing was left undone. No one knew what he was thinking about in the field, what secret efforts he was making. But what they saw was that his buckwheat grew rapidly—red stems, green leaves, sprouting swiftly, blooming beautifully.

On the height of his waist, the buckwheat looked as if it had been covered with a layer of snow. Bees buzzed all day long, collecting nectar from the flowers. Butterflies of red, yellow, and all kinds of colors fluttered and danced above the blossoms, shimmering and flashing in every direction.

People who came near the edge of the field would praise Cangjie, saying, "Cangjie is doing a great job—no buckwheat could grow better than this." Cangjie smiled happily and worked even harder. He often returned home very late, very late. Some joking people would tease him, saying, "Cangjie! You've fallen in love with buckwheat—maybe you’ve even got a romantic relationship with it!"

Cangjie just smiled and said, "Don’t say such silly things."

In truth, whenever he stepped into the buckwheat field, he truly couldn’t bear to leave.

One night, under the moonlight, the buckwheat looked even more beautiful, as if draped in a thin layer of white silk, swaying gently in the wind.

Mangsheng looked at it once, wanted to look again, looked once, wanted to look again—so absorbed was he in wonder that he forgot himself. Suddenly, at the far end of the buckwheat field, a figure flickered into view. Mangsheng thought it might be a trick of his eyes, so he rubbed his eyes and looked again—there was indeed a person. The figure was approaching, getting closer and closer, until it was right before him. Under the moonlight, he could see clearly: a young girl, about eighteen or nineteen years old, wearing green pants and a red jacket, with a pale, beautiful face. Mangsheng was surprised and asked: "It's so late at night—what are you doing here? Maybe you're just coming to ask for directions?"  

Before he could speak, the girl smiled brightly and said: "I'm sorry to trouble you so much—your care has truly made me feel uneasy."  

Mangsheng found this even more strange, and said to the girl: "You must have mistaken me for someone else."

"Daughter smiled and said, 'You've been living with me every day—how could you not know me? I'm called 'Buckwheat.' Whenever you want to find me, just call out 'Buckwheat Girl,' and I'll come right away. What a beautiful moon tonight! Do you still want to look at me for a while longer?'"

Mang Sheng nodded. The girl then waved her sleeve, and when Mang Sheng looked again, the buckwheat fields were now shining in all colors—so bright and beautiful, even the most exquisite flowers couldn't match their charm. The fragrance was so sweet, even osmanthus couldn't compare. Golden bees buzzed among the blossoms, collecting nectar, while butterflies fluttered through the air—some red as rubies, others green like emeralds—glittering and sparkling. The Buckwheat Girl spoke softly and warmly, chatting with him in a friendly, affectionate way.

It wasn't long before Mang Sheng returned home at midnight. Just as he lay down to sleep, he heard a loud wind howling outside. The wind was so strong—it shook the earth and made the houses tremble. Mang Sheng couldn't stay still. He jumped off the bed, rushed to the door, and pulled it open. His heart was pounding with anxiety. Not caring about anything else, he ran straight toward the buckwheat field.

The wind blew several times, knocking him down, and sand particles stung his face. He kept running until he reached the buckwheat field.

There, he saw something black rolling slowly toward the southwest.

He almost cried out. In the buckwheat field, there was no longer any buckwheat—only a tall dune of sand.

The wind stopped. The sky cleared and the moon shone brightly. Ma Bing shouted loudly, "Buckwheat girl, buckwheat girl..." He called her many times, but there was no trace of her. Ma Bing cried bitterly, thinking, "Maybe the buckwheat girl was caught by that black thing. Maybe she's buried under the sand dune. If the black thing took her, I will find her no matter what. If she's buried under the dune, I will dig her out myself."

The sun rose and shone upon the tall, huge sand dune. Ma Bing began digging, one shovel at a time.

Telling a story is easy, but doing it is hard. Day after day, month after month, year after year, the big sand dune slowly lowered, shrank, and finally disappeared completely.

Mangsheng was overjoyed and immediately shouted loudly:

"Qiao-mai girl!" Before the words had fully finished, there came a sudden crack, and the ground split open, shooting out a small golden sparrow.

The golden sparrow soared into the sky, glowing with light, and spoke like a human being.

"Good boy, you've rescued me—this entire store of gold and silver is all yours, for your hard work."

Mangsheng looked down and saw gold and silver rolling out endlessly from the crack in the ground.

Mangsheng stared in wonder, and then tears rolled down his cheeks. The golden sparrow noticed immediately and asked curiously, "Gold and silver, take all you want—why are you crying?"

Mangsheng looked up and asked, "Kind golden sparrow, tell me—where has the qiao-mai girl gone?"  

The golden sparrow sighed deeply and said, "The qiao-mai girl has been captured by the hook monster and taken deep into the Black Stone Cave on Mount Mota Heaven."

The monster could fly sand and stones, spew ropes to bind people. To rescue the buckwheat girl, one had to be the strongest person in the world.

Mang Sheng said, "Dear Golden Sparrow, I don't want any gold or silver. I only want to become the strongest person in the world."

Upon hearing this, Golden Sparrow fluttered down and landed right in front of Mang Sheng, then spat out a grain of golden millet.

Mang Sheng ate the golden millet. Golden Sparrow fluttered beside him and said:

"Only the most hardworking person can become the strongest person in the world."

Just as Golden Sparrow finished speaking, Mang Sheng instantly became the strongest person in the world. He said goodbye to Golden Sparrow and set off southwest to find the buckwheat girl. It was summer now, and the sun was scorching, hot, hot, burning his back. Mang Sheng saw a tree by the road—this tree was at least as thick as a person’s arm, with thick branches and leaves. He thought, "I'll just use it to shade myself."

He walked over and pulled it out with little effort. He brushed off the soil from the roots and used it as a shade umbrella, holding it as he walked forward. After walking for a while, he felt it was too heavy and awkward to carry, so he simply stuck it in his waist.

After walking for so many days, they were still about four hundred miles from Mo Tian Mountain. As soon as Meng Sheng entered the area within those four hundred miles, the Hook-Scale Demon knew it right away. It stepped out of Black Stone Cave, opened its huge mouth, and immediately blew a strong wind.

Meng Sheng was walking along when the wind carried sand and gravel down like a heavy rain. He pulled his feet out of the sand and kept going. Soon, before him stretched an endless stretch of sandy beach. The Hook-Scale Demon stood atop Mo Tian Mountain and laughed loudly.

"You want to enter my treasure land? Even if you're thirsty, I'll make you die of thirst!"

After saying this, the Hook-Scale Demon felt safe and went back into its cave to sleep.

As Meng Sheng kept walking, he began to feel thirsty. There was not a single drop of water around him. He raised his fist and punched the ground—right then, a well appeared. The well was as big as a small lake, and around it stretched at least two or three miles. Meng Sheng drank deeply, then kept walking. By the time the sun had not yet set, he had traveled four hundred miles. Even before night fell, he reached the foot of Mo Tian Mountain.

Looking up, the mountain seemed so tall it looked as if it had reached into the sky. In the middle of the mountain, dark clouds drifted by. Ma Bing tightened her belt and rolled up her sleeves, climbing upward. As she neared the Black Stone Cave, suddenly a thunderous roar rang out:

"Who dares enter my treasure ground!"

In a flash, the Hook-Scale Demon stood right before her. Its nose was a heavy weight, its eyes a bell, covered in yellow hair all over its body. Its hands were just like the hook of a scale, reaching out to grab Ma Bing. Ma Bing quickly dodged.

Seeing she couldn't catch her, the Hook-Scale Demon blew hard at the stone. A large stone, bigger than a grinding stone, shot toward Ma Bing. Ma Bing sneered and, with one hand, caught it. "You won't scare me, monster," she said. She then instantly threw the stone right back at the demon. It hit the demon's chest with a loud *puff*. The demon didn't even grunt. When the stone failed to hit Ma Bing, it opened its huge mouth and spat out a long white rope—thick as a finger—curved and hooked like a long worm, rushing toward Ma Bing.

Mangsheng's eyes were sharp and his hands quick. With a snap, he broke off a big pine tree—thick enough to wrap around two people. He held it up, shielding from left and right, and the white rope was wrapped tightly around the trunk. As the rope nearly covered the whole tree, the monster's rope wouldn't come out. Mangsheng pulled with all his might, and suddenly there was a thunderous crack—like the earth splitting open—a black heart rolled out. The monster fell to the ground, shrinking smaller and smaller, until it turned into a spider and died.

Thinking of the buckwheat girl, Mangsheng hurried into the black stone cave. There, he saw the buckwheat girl lying on the ground—already dead. Looking at her, Mangsheng's tears fell one after another.

Suddenly, a flash of golden light filled the cave, making it bright as day. Jin Xiaoqiao flew in and gently dropped a drop of water into the buckwheat girl's mouth. Instantly, she woke up. Her face was still pale, her clothes still green. Mangsheng was so happy he didn't know what to do. The buckwheat girl was joyful and grateful, saying, "You are the most hardworking and brave boy in the world. As long as you plant buckwheat there again, we'll be able to see each other again."

Mangsheng returned home and planted buckwheat in the field by the riverbank. The buckwheat grew into the finest kind.

Bees once again gathered honey, and butterflies danced there. Under the moonlight, the buckwheat girl stepped out once more.
