The Serpent Husband

At the foot of a very faraway mountain lived an old woodcutter and two daughters.

The elder daughter had a face full of freckles and didn't like hard work—she only spent her days staring into mirrors. The younger daughter was quite different: she had a sweet face and often sang like a little bird, day after day chopping wood and fetching water, busy from morning to night.

One morning, when the old woodcutter went up the mountain to cut firewood, the younger daughter said to her father, "The golden and silver flowers don't want to be worn—let's pick a flower for me to wear."

The old woodcutter returned with two large bundles of firewood. Remembering his daughter's words, he carried the wood and set out searching for flowers. After searching for a long time, he suddenly saw a beautiful little red flower just opening on the entrance of a mountain cave. The old man gasped with exhaustion and finally climbed up the cave's entrance, reached out to pluck the flower—just as he did, a roar erupted—

"Damn!" A fierce serpent monster stepped out of the cave, with green scales covering his entire body, roaring loudly, "Who dares eat bear's heart and leopard's gallbladder and steal my serpent lord's flower? This is utterly despicable!"

"Ah! I didn't know that was your flower..." The old woodcutter jumped with fright and begged for mercy.

"Say! What are you doing picking flowers for?"

"I... I... I'm picking them for my daughter..." The old woodcutter hadn't finished speaking when the serpent lord roared, "Having a daughter is easy! You must bring her to me by dawn tomorrow to marry her. Otherwise—" the serpent lord opened his mouth wide, "I'll eat you alive!"

Terrified, the old woodcutter dropped his axe, still clutching the little red flower tightly in his hand. He returned home, his daughters asking and asking until they finally understood that their father had met the terrifying serpent lord.

The eldest daughter burst into tears immediately, "I won't marry! I won't marry! The serpent lord is too scary. This is all my little sister's fault. She should be the one to marry him!"

The old woodcutter comforted her, "No, no! I won't let you marry! I'm old now. If the serpent lord eats me, it won't matter!"

The father and daughters all cried, holding each other tightly.

The little daughter thought and thought, then made up her mind. "Alright, I'll marry the serpent lord. I'll save my father."

The next morning at dawn, the little daughter climbed the mountain and married the serpent lord.

Originally, the Snake Lord was very wealthy. On the other side of the mountain, he had a palace-like home filled with blooming flowers. The tables, chairs, beds, and cabinets were all made of gold, and every pot, bowl, spoon, and plate was silver.

Although the Snake Lord was a spirit of a serpent, every night he transformed into a handsome man dressed in green silk. He was very kind to his wife, so his little daughter was even happier than before. She often wore beautiful clothes and happily returned home to visit her father and elder sister.

The elder sister saw that her younger sister had married a rich man and felt both envious and jealous. One day, she couldn’t hold back and climbed the mountain to the Snake Lord’s doorstep. At that moment, the Snake Lord had gone out, and only the little sister was at home. The elder sister smiled and praised her sister’s good fortune, then asked for things one by one: "Do you have a piece of clothing for me to wear? A skirt for me to wear? A hat for me to wear? A pair of socks for me to wear? A pair of shoes for me to wear? A set of bracelets for me to wear…?"

The generous little sister gladly handed over her clothes and jewelry, letting her elder sister wear and wear them.

The elder sister proudly dressed herself and went to the garden to pick flowers again.

When the two sisters reached the well, the elder sister suddenly had a wicked thought. She said to her younger sister, "Let's look into the well and see which one is more beautiful."

The younger sister didn't know her sister's true intentions. She bent down and looked into the well water. At that moment, the elder sister pushed her hard, and suddenly a loud "plink!" echoed—poor little sister fell straight into the well.

The elder sister laughed and went back to the house, pretending to be her younger sister, waiting for the Snake Husband to come home. When the Snake Husband saw a woman with a crooked face, he asked curiously, "Who are you?"

"I'm your wife!" the sister replied.

"But your feet are so big!"

"I walked through the forest and my feet grew big."

"Your body is so long!"

"I picked fruits and my body grew long."

"You have so many freckles on your face!"

"Got scratched by thorns!"

Just as the two were talking, their younger sister turned into a beautiful little bird and flew out of the well, flapping her wings. She sang, "Big sister wears my clothes, shy-shy—big sister wears my skirt, shy-shy—big sister wears my hat, shy-shy—" As she sang, she flew right onto the serpent lord's palm. The serpent lord looked down at the bird and felt very strange, so he put the little bird in a golden cage.

When the elder sister heard the bird scolding her, she killed the bird and cooked it into a dish, placing it on the table. But when they were eating, the serpent lord always picked up meat with his chopsticks, while the elder sister's chopsticks only brought bones.

The elder sister was furious. She threw the bones into the garden. Soon, the bones grew into a big hawthorn tree, full of red hawthorns. The serpent lord picked one and tasted it—sweet! But the elder sister's picked ones were always sour.

The elder sister was even more furious. She cut down the hawthorn tree and used it as a threshold. The serpent lord stepped over it easily, but the elder sister always slipped and fell, "gurp!" and landed on her back, four feet in the air.

The elder sister was truly furious!

They took down the threshold and threw it into the furnace. The flames burst out, splashing sparks onto her face, and from that day on, her sister's face became a big, colorful mask.

At that moment, she was so angry! She grabbed a bucket of water and was about to pour it into the stove, but when she opened the stove door, she saw a hot, steaming glutinous rice cake inside. So she put the rice cake into her bedclothes to warm it up, thinking she'd secretly eat it later alone. But by nightfall, she completely forgot about the rice cake in the bedclothes.

That night, when the Snake Lord was about to go to sleep, he lifted the blanket and found the rice cake had turned into his beautiful wife. The Snake Lord was surprised and asked, "How do you look so much like my wife?"

"I not only look like her—I am her!" At that moment, the Snake Lord finally understood everything that had happened was all his sister's trick.

When the sister saw her little sister return, she felt ashamed and lowered her head, saying, "Sister, I have no face to see you. Please forgive me!" And then she jumped into the well.
