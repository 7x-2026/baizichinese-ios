Ling Lun Invents Music — A Legend of Phoenix Ridge

"Qiao Shan moonlight gathers beauty, Phoenix Ridge's spring mist wraps the eight directions." Phoenix Ridge is located to the east of the Yellow Emperor's tomb and is one of the eight scenic spots in Huangling County. It is said that Phoenix Ridge is where phoenixes land. Once, the Yellow Emperor ordered Ling Lun to create musical scales. Ling Lun collected bamboo from Xie Valley, first making bamboo tubes with even thickness and uniformity. At first, the sounds produced were without yin and yang distinctions and could not form a proper musical scale. People mocked Ling Lun, saying, "Your bamboo tubes are useless unless you blow them—once you blow, even wild animals are frightened away." One day, while the Yellow Emperor was practicing riding a horse, he had just mounted the horse when a strange sound from Ling Lun's bamboo tubes suddenly echoed. The horse heard the odd sound, panicked, leaped into the air, reared up, and neighed loudly, throwing the Yellow Emperor off the horse. Ling Lun quickly ran over and helped lift the Yellow Emperor up. The Yellow Emperor said to Ling Lun, "This small bamboo tube you made is powerful enough to scare my horse—clearly it's no ordinary thing. In the future, it must be able to produce beautiful music." Hearing the Yellow Emperor's encouragement, Ling Lun humbly said to him, "I have failed to create a proper musical scale for three years—this is already a great mistake. Yet the Yellow Emperor still encourages me."

The Yellow Emperor said, "You can't say that like this. A simple bamboo tube with a few small holes drilled into it can produce sound—this is your invention and achievement. How can it be called 'a sin'?" Saying this, he rode away on his horse.

Encouraged by the Yellow Emperor, Ling Lun became even more confident and practiced tirelessly every day, but still couldn't produce harmonious tones. One day, Ling Lun went alone to Phoenix Ridge, lay down on a stone, and thought deeply. Without realizing it, he fell asleep. As he was sleeping soundly, he was suddenly awakened by a beautiful bird song from the trees. Ling Lun quickly sat up and rubbed his eyes. Looking up, he saw two beautiful birds with graceful bodies and splendid feathers singing. Their voices were soft and melodious, incredibly pleasant to hear. Ling Lun stared wide-eyed, listening carefully, and without thinking, picked up his homemade bamboo tube and began to imitate the birds' calls. Just as he was playing enthusiastically, the two birds suddenly stopped singing and took flight, flying away. Ling Lun was left in panic, stamping his feet and waving his hands, but the birds had vanished without a trace.

After returning, Ling Lun reported the matter to the Yellow Emperor and then blew for him a half-learned, broken version of the bird call he had heard. The Yellow Emperor listened and said happily, "This bird is the Phoenix, the king among birds. The fact that the Bridge Nation can attract the Phoenix is a sign of good fortune." From then on, the place where the Phoenix rested was called "Fengling." Every day, Ling Lun went to Fengling and sat on a large stone, waiting for the Phoenix to call out. Indeed, the forests of Fengling saw many Phoenixes landing. However, not every Phoenix that landed would call. After long observation, Ling Lun discovered that when the Phoenixes called, the male Phoenix's call was passionate and energetic, while the female Phoenix's call was soft and melodious. Whenever a pair of Phoenixes landed, each would call six times, then together they called once in unison before flying away. Based on the two sets of six calls from the Phoenixes, Ling Lun carefully studied and thought deeply for a long time, and finally created the 12 tones of music. The Yellow Emperor praised him for this achievement. Afterward, Ling Lun recorded the calls of all kinds of birds and animals, continuously enriching the music system he had created.

For example, beating a drum can represent the sound of a horse's hooves running; using a whistle can imitate the calls of different birds. Some people say that the notes used in modern music—1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7—originated from the Chinese people, possibly from Ling Lun's system of musical scales, even though the notes were written differently back then.
