A Blue Dragon

The dragon is the only imaginary animal among the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac.

Long ago, many winged dragons lived on the earth, and they were friends with human beings. These great dragons willingly guarded people’s palaces and carried people on their travels. From childhood, people loved riding on the dragons’ warm, broad wings. By the time those people grew old, the dragons were still young. Dragons lived for a very long time and knew many things that people did not know.

One day, someone wondered, “Why do people die while dragons seem to live forever?” A rumor began to spread: anyone who drank a dragon’s blood could live forever too. People began killing dragons, and fewer and fewer dragons remained on earth.

But they had not all been killed. Seeing that people had become unfriendly, many dragons left the earth to live among the stars of the Pleiades.

Even so, the dragons still loved human beings. They often visited the earth, but no longer appeared in the shape of dragons. Instead, they came as invisible wind, as rain, or as rainbows. Sometimes they appeared in people’s dreams, helping them through difficult times.

Some dragons could not bear to leave the earth. They made themselves invisible and lived with the mountains, rivers, and oceans. But people cut into mountains, built dams and roads, and dug mines. In the story, these works often cut through the dragons’ lifelines, and soon droughts, floods, or earthquakes followed.

Other dragons turned themselves into tiny snakes or lizards and hid among bushes and blades of grass. But people often caught them and kept them in little containers to look at. Trapped in those small jars, the creatures often died for lack of air.

It is said that if the whole earth ever faces a great crisis, the dragons living in the Pleiades will appear in the sky. They will rescue the kind people of the earth, carry them on their wings, and take them to live on a distant world.
