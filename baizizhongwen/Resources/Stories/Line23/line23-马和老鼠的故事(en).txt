The Horse and the Mouse

Once, a mouse lived in a hole in the wall of a house. He had lived there for many years and knew all the tasty food in the kitchen and all the interesting things in the drawers. Every night at midnight, he stole food from the kitchen and toys from the children’s drawers and hid them in his hole.

After losing things again and again, the family finally decided to pull down the house and move elsewhere. The mouse had to move too. He stole two sacks from the family, filled them with food and toys, and left on the night before his hole was to be destroyed.

Now he had no home. First, he needed to find a friend who would let him stay.

He thought of the ox, but quickly changed his mind. Just as the Jade Emperor was about to declare the ox the first animal of the zodiac, the mouse had cleverly jumped onto the ox’s horns and taken first place. The ox still resented him for it.

Next he thought of the tiger, but at once imagined a terrible, hungry roar. Then he thought of the rabbit, who ate grass all day. Such plain food would never suit him. He had heard of the dragon, but had no idea where the dragon lived.

Then he remembered the horse, whom he admired most of all. The horse was so tall and handsome, and even faster than he was!

The mouse decided to try his luck at the stable near the village. Carrying his two sacks of food and toys, he arrived at the stable door, dripping with sweat, and knocked.

“Brother Horse, my family is pulling down the house. May I stay with you for a few days?”

When the horse heard that it was the mouse, he answered with annoyance, “You bustle about all night instead of sleeping. I would not want to share my home with you. I have a race early tomorrow morning.”

“Look! I have brought you tasty food and wonderful toys,” said the mouse, opening the sacks.

The horse was astonished to see so much food and so many clever toys. Suddenly, he understood why the family wanted to pull down the house.

“You stole all these things from your family, didn’t you? I do not want to live with a thief!” With that, the horse kicked the two sacks and the mouse out of the door.

The mouse had to spend the night in a hollow tree outside the stable. He felt sad and sorry for what he had done. He let the wind scatter the food and toys from his sacks.

Early the next morning, the horse set off for a race in the hills. The mouse followed him.

The horse was far ahead of the other racers when he suddenly stumbled into a trap. A net closed around him, binding his legs so tightly that he could not get free.

The mouse gnawed with all his strength at the ropes of the net. At last, the horse was free. He ran on and won first place.

The mouse jumped for joy and cheered for him. The horse invited the mouse to ride on his back and carried him home to the stable. From then on, they were good friends.
