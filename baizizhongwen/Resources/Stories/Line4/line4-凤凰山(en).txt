Phoenix Mountain

Long, long ago, at the foot of a mountain south of West Lake, lived two siblings, a brother named Chunsheng and a sister named Qiugu. When their parents died, they were left with three mu of rented farmland and a broken thatch hut. The brother and sister worked from dawn till midnight, tilling the land and hoping to grow enough food. But when the grain ripened in autumn, the rich landlords came to collect rent. After paying the rent, what remained was not enough to eat. With no choice, the two lived on thin soup and thin porridge, enduring hardship day after day.

One New Year's Eve, the wealthy landlords' house was filled with chicken, duck, fish, and meat. In Chunsheng's home, only a small cup of rice remained. Qiugu boiled a thin bowl of porridge and offered it to her brother. Chunsheng said, "I'm not hungry, sister, you have it instead!" Qiugu replied, "I'm not hungry, brother, you have it instead!" The two pushed and pulled, neither willing to drink it.

At that moment, snowflakes like goose feathers fell from the sky, and the wind howled fiercely from the north, making one shiver. In the wind and snow came an old beggar woman. Her hair was white, her clothes tattered, and she held a walking stick, stumbling from step to step, calling out as she walked, "Oh, North Wind! Oh, white snow ground!"

Kind-hearted people, please pity me, old granny!" This hoarse voice reached the broken thatch hut, and both siblings heard it clearly. Qiu Gu said, "Brother, how pitiful that sounds!" Chun Sheng said, "Sister, let's invite her in." Saying that, he quickly opened the door and helped the strange old woman into the house. Qiu Gu hurriedly brushed off the snow from her clothes, while Chun Sheng brought the bowl of thin porridge that had been passed around for so long and offered it to her.

The old woman stayed in their home for one night. The next day, the snow stopped and the sky cleared. She got up to say goodbye. As she was leaving, the old woman took out a piece of white silk and gave it to Qiu Gu, saying, "Girl, use your skillful hands to embroider this piece of white silk. Happiness is destined for those who are hardworking and kind." Qiu Gu took it and looked closely—on the white silk, faintly drawn was a phoenix.

So Qiu Gu embroidered the silk day and night. She used red silk thread to stitch the phoenix's head, black silk thread for its eyes, golden silk thread for its wings, and colorful silk threads for its tail.

Xiao Chen kept stitching, and the embroidery needle pierced her finger, blood staining the white silk. She then stitched a red sun and fluffy clouds upon it. From the beginning of spring to the beginning of summer, she finally completed the phoenix.

This phoenix picture was truly beautiful—the phoenix raised its head toward the fiery sun above, as if alive. The two siblings hung it in their house, growing more and more delighted, more and more fond of it. One night, strange things happened. Qiu Gu woke up in the middle of the night and saw a golden glow filling the room. Looking closely, she saw the phoenix step down from the picture. She called her brother awake, and the two siblings watched silently. The phoenix walked around the room several times, then returned to the white silk, and the golden light disappeared.

The next morning, Qiu Gu got up to sweep the floor and found a golden phoenix egg on the ground. The two siblings sold it and bought several acres of land and a yellow cow. People say, "Good things spread far and wide." The story of the phoenix picture spread like a wind, reaching the ears of the county magistrate.

The county magistrate thought: "This phoenix picture that produces golden eggs is a rare treasure! I must have it!" So he called Spring-Sheng over and said, "Because of your favor, I'm offering you 300 taels of silver for your phoenix picture."

Spring-Sheng replied, "The phoenix picture was painstakingly embroidered by my sister. We won't sell it!"

Hearing this, the magistrate's face darkened. He slammed the wooden gavel and said, "This is clearly a treasure of the emperor! How could a poor family possibly create such a magnificent picture?" Without hesitation, he accused Spring-Sheng of stealing national treasure and put him in prison. At the same time, he ordered the officials to seize the phoenix picture from Spring-Sheng's home.

When the phoenix picture was taken, the magistrate was absolutely delighted. He looked at it from left to right, forgot to eat, and laughed loudly. That night, he sat in his official chair, waiting for the phoenix to lay a golden egg. Late at night, the phoenix picture suddenly emitted a brilliant light, shining brightly throughout the room. And indeed, the phoenix came down from the picture.

He thought the phoenix was about to lay an egg, so he quickly bent down to look. But instead, the phoenix charged at him with fierce energy, pecking wildly and blindly. Painful and frightened, he rolled around on the ground, screaming, "Help! Save me!" The officials rushed over, but by then the phoenix had suddenly burst through the window and flown up to the mountain. The officials lifted the county magistrate up, and found him covered in blood, one eye completely blinded from the pecks.

The county magistrate, still not satisfied, thought: "That girl could draw this phoenix picture so beautifully—she must be able to draw another one." So he called out to Qiu Gu, and told her: "If you can redraw the phoenix picture again, I'll set Spring Sheng free." To save her brother, Qiu Gu agreed. She took back the white silk from the magistrate, and stitched the phoenix picture one thread at a time for three months. The beautiful phoenix was finished again—but one pair of phoenix eyes remained uncompleted. She told the magistrate: "I want my brother released first, and then I'll finish stitching the phoenix eyes."

The magistrate ordered the officials to free Spring Sheng. When Qiu Gu saw her brother, she stitched the phoenix eyes in one swift stroke.

The phoenix had eyes, so it spread its wings and flew down, carrying the two siblings and flying up to the top of the mountain, where it disappeared.

Later, people called this mountain "Phoenix Mountain."
