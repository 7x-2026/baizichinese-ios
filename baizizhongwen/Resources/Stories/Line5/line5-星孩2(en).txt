The Star Child, Part Two

Long ago, there were two boys who had been best friends since childhood. One was very intelligent and did well in school, while the other was not bright at all. His friend helped him learn everything, but the less intelligent boy still couldn't learn. So he lived a hard life in that area.

The clever boy grew up and was about to take the exam when he died. According to village custom, a person must marry and have children, so the less intelligent boy took on the role of son for the dead friend's family. Soon, he began to feel bored and told himself: "My friend's talent won't help me—everything will fade away. I must learn how to survive on my own." So he became a boatman, making a living by transporting goods. One day, he tied his boat to the riverbank of a small town, and a big boy walked over and sat down beside him. That big boy looked as if he were starving—very hungry.

The boy felt sorry for him, so he bought food for him. He ate it all quickly. The boy then bought another portion, but the big boy said it wasn't enough—he wanted to eat all the ham and cookies, the big bread too. The boy gave him everything. After eating, the big boy felt full and they became good friends.

The little boy noticed that the big boy looked sad and down, so he wanted to ask him why. The big boy treated the little boy as a true friend and promised he would never hurt him or make him suffer. The little boy asked where the big boy's home was. The big boy said, "I have no home on land, no boat on the sea. I live in the village by day and in the town at night." At first, the little boy didn't understand what he meant. One day, when they had to cross the river, the boat broke and sank. Both of them fell into the water. The big boy quickly saved the little boy and put him in a safe place. He repaired the boat and then put the little boy back in. They continued their journey and carried goods.

The little boy was naturally respectful of others. They had spent a long time together. One day, the little boy accidentally asked the big boy, "If someone could go up to the sky, would they be able to know what happens in the sky?" The big boy replied, "Maybe you could go right into the sky." So the little boy fell asleep, and when he woke up, he saw stars above his head, like lotuses blooming in heaven. He even could pick these flowers and put them in his pocket. Then he saw a big ship arrive—driven by a dragon—and inside was a bottle of water. At that moment, the little boy took the bottle and sat with the big boy on the clouds. The big boy first tried pouring the water onto the ground, and when it touched the earth, it started to rain. The little boy realized they could work like air spirits. He asked the big boy to pour the water onto the soil of his hometown, and then he tied a rope to go back down to the ground.

The big boy told him, "I am the son of the Thunder God. My duty is to bring rain to the blessed lands. For a while, I failed in my duty, so I was punished and sent down to the earth. You saved me." Then he sent the boy back to the ground, back to his homeland. And he placed the stars he had picked from heaven on the table, and the whole room shone with light. The stars emitted light, even allowing people to read them—but during the day, they looked like ordinary metal stones, only glowing at night.

One day, the little boy's wife noticed this. She used the light to comb her hair, but the stone became very unhappy and turned into a small piece. One day, her wife had a sudden idea—she wanted to swallow the stone. When she swallowed it, something strange happened. She became pregnant and gave birth to a boy. And the big boy truly became the son of the little boy—especially because this boy could glow like a star at night. Everyone called him the Star Child.

Star Child grew up and became very talented, quickly becoming an important person in the little boy's life.
