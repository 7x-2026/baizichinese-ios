The Legend of the Sun Stone

Little friends, last night I had a dream. In my dream, I was climbing Phoenix Ridge. After passing the Taoyuan Pavilion, I began climbing the Sky Ladder. Halfway up the Sky Ladder, I came across a magical cave. Inside, I saw an old man with white hair meditating. As I passed by, he suddenly opened his eyes and gestured for me to sit down. He said he had something important to tell me. Without thinking, I sat down. He told me that he was Grandpa Wei of Phoenix Ridge—the one who had once achieved immortality and become a immortal in ancient times. He said he was preparing a story for children, and asked me, "How do you know there are little friends here?" He replied, "Over the past two months, I've heard the songs of parents, teachers, and children every day. I know that school is about to start. This story is specially for the first-grade students of Chun Valley. It's a gift for them."

Here it is—the story that Grandpa Wei of Phoenix Ridge is telling you.  

A long, long time ago, in the deep and vast Purple Weave Palace, there lived a dragon stone. Originally, it was a round, colorful gemstone created by Nüwa when she repaired the sky. It was a Sun Stone.

After a long sleep without dreams, the seed inside the stone slowly sprouted and grew leaves. After another long sleep filled with dreams, the leaves gradually turned into fins. The fins slid through the moonlight and slowly left the water. In the glow of light, they became wings. From that moment on, this angel with wings could sing like a seven-note harp of sunlight, and fly like a peacock spreading its seven-colored feathers.

He traveled to Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Earth, and Mercury, and even to the Sun and the Moon. Yet he never once reached Earth—the small village by the Yellow River where he was born. He longed for the East, and longed to return home. His name was Sun Sidong. Grandpa Phoenix Ridge continued, saying that a long, long time ago, the universe was chaotic, like a big egg. Inside that egg, the first life appeared. His name was Pangu. Pangu slept for many years. When he woke up, everything around him was dark.

He picked up an axe and swung it around, and suddenly a loud crack echoed—the egg split open. The light elements rose upward and became the sky, while the heavy elements sank downward and became the earth. Worried that heaven and earth would come back together, Pangu used his hands to support the sky and his legs to hold the earth.

As time passed, the sky grew higher and higher, the earth grew thicker and thicker, and Pangu’s body grew longer and longer—until he became a giant, stretching from heaven to earth.

Many years passed, and Pangu grew too old and finally died. After his death, great changes happened around him. His eyes became the sun and the moon. His hair became the stars. His breath became wind and clouds. His voice became thunder and lightning. His shoulders and arms became mountains. His blood became rivers. His limbs spread out to form the four directions. His five senses became wood, fire, earth, metal, and water. His body hair became grass, bamboo, insects, fish, birds, and animals. His teeth and bones became colorful gems.

After Pangu created heaven and earth, a war broke out between the Fire God Zhurong and the Water God Gonggong. The Water God lost the battle and felt deeply ashamed. He smashed his head against a mountain ridge—this was one of the four pillars supporting the sky and earth. In one instant, the pillar broke, and the western sky collapsed. Rivers of water poured down from the heavens, flooding the earth, making it a land of suffering, wild beasts, and chaos. At that moment, the great Nüwa appeared. She borrowed the four legs of a giant turtle to support the four ridges of the sky. She gathered colorful stones from the banks of the Yellow River and piled them into a rainbow mountain. She built layers of reeds and set them on fire. The fire burned for nine days and nine nights, and finally, the colorful stones melted into one. Nüwa then collected seven colors from Jupiter, Mars, Saturn, Venus, Mercury, the Sun, and the Moon, and poured them into the molten rock. Then, she gathered a seed from a tree on the eastern Fusu tree and planted it in the soil. The molten rock transformed into a seven-colored sun stone. Nüwa lifted this magical stone and repaired the broken, leaking holes in the sky. With this sun stone, Nüwa saved the wounded world.

After Nüwa mended the sky, the Sun Stone was taken to the distant and profound Purple Palace. Inside this Sun Stone, there lies an ancient seed of the East, which had slept for 49,000 years in the faraway old Saturn. Only after the Sun separated from Saturn did this seed awaken and sprout. But the angel's wings remained like hibiscus flowers, still sleeping. It slept another 49,000 years, until a thunderclap rang out, in flames and mist, as the old Sun broke free from Saturn. Then, its hibiscus leaves began to glide like fins through the waters of the Milky Way.

After another 4,900 years, during a tremendous storm of lightning and thunder, its fins transformed into wings, and it soared through the light ether of heaven. It became a shining, winged dragon angel. The seven-colored patterns on its body recorded the paths of all things in the universe. Its voice sang through every song in the world. Its seven-colored wings spread across the sun, moon, and stars.

He taught all the creation stories in the all-powerful language of the universe. He could speak the language of the sun and the moon, communicating with nebulae, storms, birds, and trees. The only regret was that he had never returned to Earth—the place by the Yellow River where he was born. "I want to go back to Earth," Sun Sidong begged his mother.

"If you return to Earth, you will first lose your wings, and from then on you will no longer be able to fly—you will have to walk on your legs. You will also lose your colorful dragon pattern, the shimmering light and design of heaven. Under its glow, you would never get lost. But on Earth, even though you have eyes, you will constantly wander in darkness. In the end, you will lose your universal language—you will no longer be able to understand the sounds of wind and water, nor read the messages written in stars and birds." Nüwa told Sun Sidong.

"But the only way to regain your wings, your dragon pattern, and your language of the universe is to find your twenty little friends across the five great lands within twelve years. You must listen to their stories, learn every language they speak, and share with them all the stories and languages you remember."

"Yes, I can do it," Sun Sidong said firmly. So Nuwa drew gifts from Saturn, the Sun, the Moon, and the stars, transforming them into sound, color, and lines, and created a rainbow. You can slide down this rainbow to reach the ground, Sun Sidong said hesitantly, "But on Earth, I'll miss you. What if I think of you?" "Whenever you sing, play, draw, or tell stories with the children, I will be with you," Nuwa told Sun Sidong.

Saying this, she took out a round stone from her chest. The stone was as round as a mirror, and in the center, faintly visible, was a dragon with wings. Under the sunlight, the stone shimmered, and the dragon could be seen flying through transparent cloud patterns. She handed it to Sun Sidong and said, "Wear it. When you face difficulties, turn the mirror toward the sun, and the flying dragon will help you remove all worries."

Sun Sidong took the mirror. It felt familiar to him. He held it tightly and excitedly ran along the beautiful rainbow all the way down to the ground. Just as he touched the earth, a loud crash sounded—the colorful mirror shattered. A dragon burst out from the broken pieces. Oh no. Sun Sidong's heart broke. He rushed to piece the black fragments together, but it didn’t work. He kept trying and trying. All day long, he still couldn’t restore it. Yet in the process, many magical patterns appeared.

For the first time, he formed a bird. The bird sang the first song.

The second time he arranged the stones and formed two tigers, who traveled through the forest day and night. The third time, he made three ants carrying rice under the starlight. The fourth time, he created a dragon, a tiger, a bird, and a turtle. The fifth time, he formed five animals: tiger, deer, pig, rabbit, and mouse. These were the songs he later taught to first-grade children.

The next day, he woke up from a dream, remembering ancient Eastern tales of the hero Qufu chasing the sun and Jingwei filling the sea. He also followed Yu the Great through strange lands—Wonderland, the One-Eye Nation, the Nie Er Nation, the Tongue-Changing Nation, and the Three-Headed Nation. He arranged the images of each one with the stones.

Later, during a midday dream, he traveled to Europe and heard Aesop tell many fables. He saw Saint Francis the Holy, who told stories about wolves, and with the stones he formed that wolf.

These were the stories he later told to second-grade children. On the third day, he still couldn’t make a complete circle, but he formed a large boat. The boat was full of animals, plants, and people. This Noah’s Ark arrived safely in the East, under a rainbow.

Later he went to Egypt and heard Joseph tell about a dream in which he and his brother were bundling wheat in a field. His wheat stood upright, while his brother knelt around it in submission. This dream came true, for at that time the world faced a great famine, and the brother went to Egypt to seek help from his younger brother. Later, when Sun Sidong returned to China, people had no food to eat and were even eating dirt. At that time, a divine dragon with horns came up with a solution. He collected seeds and planted them, and invented a tool called "lei" to teach people how to plow the land. Sun Sidong also traveled with the people to markets, where he saw many ancient units of measurement and weights. These were the stories Sun Sidong would later tell to third-grade students.

On the fourth night, Sun Sidong dreamed of the gods of Norway, who began a long and endless war that lasted until the end of the world, and a new sun rose in the east.

The deities in the east were also fighting fiercely in the deep mountains and forests, until heaven began to assign gods to manage each of the four directions—this is the story of the Four Directional Gods that Sun Sidong would later tell to fourth graders.

On the fifth day, he still couldn't complete the perfect circle. Tired from working so hard, he fell into a deep sleep. In his dream, he saw a lotus flower with six petals. Each petal extended into a path, and he followed one leading west. He walked through the desert and saw two monks. Following them, he entered India. In a monk's kingdom in India, he drank magic water and lost all his memories. He sat under a bodhi tree and meditated for six days and six nights, and finally understood the way back.

On the sixth day, Sun Sidong managed to sketch an ancient city gate and then fell asleep. In his dream, he saw a north star and a city in the west. A man from the west told him: "You must pass through seven sealed city gates to find your Sun Stone. To open each gate, you must use weapons made from seven metals—gold, silver, copper, iron, tin, lead, and another metal you haven't yet discovered."

The first door is called the Door of Forgetting, leading to India. It can only be opened with a key made of gold.

The second door is called the Door of Darkness, leading to Persia. It can only be opened with a key as bright as silver.

The third door is called the Door of Death, leading to Egypt. It can only be opened with a key made of copper.

The fourth door is called the Door of Angels, leading to Greece. It can only be opened with a key made of iron.

This is the story he later told to fifth-grade children.

The fifth door is called the Door of Warfare, leading to Rome. It can only be opened with a key made of tin.

This is the story Sun Sidong will tell to sixth-grade students.

The sixth door is called the Door of Destruction, leading to Germany. It can only be opened with a key made of lead.

This is the story he will later tell to seventh-grade students.

The seventh door is called the Door of Freedom, leading to America. It can only be opened with a key made of alloy.

This is the story Sun Sidong will tell to eighth-grade students.

In this long dream, Sun Sidong endured great hardships, and after fighting the demon with seven weapons, he bled so much that he fell into unconsciousness. When he woke up, he looked at the North Star, and instantly the seven weapons turned into seven golden keys. He finally opened the seven great gates, but still did not see the Sun Stone. Then Sun Sidong found himself lying by the banks of the Yellow River. He stood up, and all the broken pieces of the Sun Stone disappeared. At that moment, a group of dragon horses swam up from the riverbed and brought him a precious box. When he opened it, a magical map made of black and white dot patterns unfolded before him. A familiar figure emerged from the waves of the Yellow River and the breath of the dragons.

A beautiful picture of text appeared from bamboo leaves, fish fins, and phoenix feathers—a magical and ancient language. This voice told Sun Sidong that if he wanted to find his Sun Stone and learn the language of the universe, and to discover the world and its pictures, the only way was to cross five continents and seven islands, travel all over the world, find his twenty friends, listen to every story and song they have, and in return, share his own stories and songs with them.

That very night, Sun Sidong disappeared by the Yellow River. Where did he go? Grandpa Phoenix Ridge stopped telling his story.

Where did Sun Sidong go? No one knows. He wasn't in the Sun Palace in the sky. Look—before it rains, the rainbow is always broken. So Sun Sidong is among us. In the morning, he rests in the dewdrops; at night, he flies into children's dreams.

Little friends, Sun Sidong is a red-robed flying hero. He is searching for his twenty little friends. Would you like to see him in your dreams?

You've all had many dreams, and you can't remember them clearly, but there's one thing that's certain: if someone can tell the story or draw the story the next day, then they have truly seen Sun Sidong, one of his twenty friends. Do you want to meet Sun Sidong? All you need to do is hold a pencil and enter your dream, and when you draw the story from your dream, you'll have seen Sun Sidong. And if you then tell the story you drew, and share it with Sun Sidong's dream, you will become one of his twenty friends. He will invite you to go on a journey to find the Sun Stone, and you will all gather at Chunzi Valley in the west of Beijing. There, you'll sing, play games, draw pictures, and tell stories together.

But in that happy dream, you aren't really you, and Sun Sidong isn't a little boy in red clothes. He might be a herdsboy holding a wooden flute, and you could be a little squirrel or a small rabbit dancing to the rhythm of the song. When the story ends, Grandpa Phoenix Ridge still asks me to pass on some gifts to the children. Among these gifts, you'll find some amazing discoveries. Later, when we go into the classroom, we'll give each child their gift.
