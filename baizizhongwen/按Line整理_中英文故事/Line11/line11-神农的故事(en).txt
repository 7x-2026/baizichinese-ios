The Story of Shennong

After the time of Fuxi, people hunted, fished, gathered fruits, and survived on grass, roots, insects, fish, birds, and animals. But as the population grew, food became scarce, and people often went hungry.

One day, a woman met the farming god and became pregnant. She gave birth to a boy with a cow's head and human body. The boy could speak within three hours, walk within five days, have teeth by seven days, and by nine days, nine dry wells began to flow with water. While gathering fruits, the boy discovered that seeds, when dropped to the ground, took root and grew into plants. He imitated nature, collecting seeds and planting them. The seeds sprouted, bloomed, and produced fruit. A red bird flew overhead and delivered rice seeds to the boy. He scattered the rice seeds into a paddy field. The rice grew into seedlings, then into stalks, then into ears of grain, which were ground into white rice. The white rice was cooked into porridge. People called this boy Shennong. Shennong also taught people how to farm, made farming tools such as plow, rake, seed drill, sickle, and hoe; he taught people to plant six grains according to the seasons: rice, millet, foxtail millet, wheat, barley, and beans; and he taught people to raise six types of livestock: cattle, horses, sheep, chickens, dogs, and pigs.

Shennong also transformed the Three Principles into the Six Harmonies, and evolved the Eight Trigrams of Fu Xi into the 64 Trigrams. From this, he invented the ancient lunar calendar, which showed how to use the 64 Trigrams to represent the 12 hours of a day and the 12 months and 24 solar terms of a year. Thus, Shennong founded the age of agriculture.

Shennong was not only the god of grains, but also the god of medicine. One day, he accidentally discovered that hawthorn fruit could relieve stomach bloating, and from this he gained inspiration—plants, trees, herbs, fruits could all be used to treat illness. He decided to taste hundreds of herbs. Every day, he went deep into the mountains and forests to collect medicine, carefully observing the roots, stems, leaves, and fruits of plants. He tasted all the flavors—sour, sweet, bitter, spicy, salty—and even poison. He would get poisoned several times a day. Moved by Shennong’s spirit, the Mountain God transformed his bamboo whip, which once startled the wild grasses, into a divine whip. With this divine whip, Shennong struck the herbs. Strong ones turned red, cold ones turned white, warm ones turned yellow, and poisonous ones turned black. He then made herbal remedies such as soups, pills, ointments, and pills, and gave them to different patients. To heal more people, Shennong continued tasting herbs every day, until he finally died from the deadly poison of the "cutting intestines" herb.
