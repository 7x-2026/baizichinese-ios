The Yellow Emperor Casts a Tripod

The Yellow Emperor wandered everywhere, ordering people to mine copper from Shoushan and transport it to the foot of Jingshan, where they would cast a great tripod to commemorate his victory over Chiyou. It was not, as some people imagined, used for refining elixirs of immortality. The Yellow Emperor was already a god, and did not need to imitate later Daoist sages practicing alchemy. However, because he fought a great battle with Chiyou at Zhuolu, killed Chiyou, and then stayed on earth for some time before returning to heaven, some people wrongly believed that he first practiced Daoism and then became a immortal—this is actually incorrect.

As for the magnificent tripod cast at the foot of Jingshan, it was finally completed. During the process of casting, tigers, leopards, and birds from the sky all came to guard the furnace and protect the fire. This was a huge tripod, standing one zhang and three chi high, with a capacity larger than a large ceramic jar that could hold ten grains. Around the tripod, dragons soaring through clouds were carved—likely the Yinglong—along with four-faced spirits and various strange birds and beasts.

The Yellow Emperor placed this sacred cauldron at the foot of Jing Mountain and held a grand celebration to mark its completion. Heavenly deities and people from all directions came to attend, making the gathering truly bustling and magnificent.

As the festive ceremony was underway, suddenly a divine dragon, its scales shimmering with golden light, emerged from the clouds, lowering half of its body and letting its whiskers dangle down to touch the cauldron. The Yellow Emperor knew that the messenger sent to welcome him back to heaven had arrived. So, he took along seventy heavenly gods who had descended with him, leapt into the clouds, and mounted the dragon's back, rising gently toward the heavens. Below, the common people and officials saw the Yellow Emperor riding the dragon into the sky and all wanted to follow him. Unable to climb onto the dragon's back, they rushed forward and began pulling at its whiskers. The whiskers could not bear the force of so many people pulling, and they fell one by one. Even the precious bow that the Yellow Emperor had hung on the whiskers was pulled down and fell to the ground.

The king and common people who fell to the ground clutched the bow or held onto the dragon's whiskers, crying out in sorrow. Later, that bow was called the "Wu Bow," and that place was named "Ding Hu," meaning "the dragon's whisker on the sacred tripod." The term "Ding Hu" literally means "the whiskers of the tripod dragon," and if written as "Ding Hu" in some books, it makes no sense. As for the whiskers that fell down, it is said that they later grew into grass—today's dragon whisker grass.
