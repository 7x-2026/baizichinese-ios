The Yellow Emperor Builds a South-Pointing Chariot

The invention of the south-pointing chariot dates back to a legend from 5,000 years ago, about the Yellow Emperor's war against Chiyou. It is said that during three years of fighting, the Yellow Emperor and Chiyou engaged in seventy-two battles but still couldn't win. In the battle at Zhuolu (originally described as "the great battle in Jizhou"), when Chiyou was about to lose, he summoned Feng Bo and Yu Shi, the gods of wind and rain, who called storms and rain, making it difficult for the Yellow Emperor's army to advance. The Yellow Emperor quickly sent for a goddess named Han Bao from heaven, who used magic to stop the wind and rain, allowing the army to keep moving forward. But Chiyou, clever and full of tricks, then released a thick fog that spread everywhere, making the army lose their way. The Yellow Emperor became very worried and ordered the army to stop advancing and stay in place. He immediately gathered his ministers to discuss a plan. Ministers such as Shennong, Changxian, Dahan, and Limei all arrived, but Fenghou was nowhere to be seen. Some people suspected that Fenghou might have been killed by Chiyou. The Yellow Emperor immediately sent people to search everywhere, but after searching for a long time, no trace of him was found.

The Yellow Emperor had no choice but to go himself. When he arrived at the battlefield, he saw Feng Hou sleeping alone on the war chariot. The Yellow Emperor angrily said, "What time is it! How can you still be sleeping here?" Feng Hou slowly stood up and replied, "I'm not sleeping—I'm actually thinking of a solution." Then, he pointed his hand toward the sky and said to the Yellow Emperor, "Look! Why does the Big Dipper in the sky rotate, but its handle stays still? I've heard that Bo Gao discovered a magnetic stone during his copper-smelting work on the mountain, which can attract iron. Could we use this principle to create something that points in a fixed direction? With such a device, we would never lose our way." The Yellow Emperor shared this idea with his ministers, and after discussing it, everyone agreed it was a good plan. Then Feng Hou designed the device, and all the people worked together to build it. After several days and nights of hard work, they created something that could show direction. Feng Hou installed it on a war chariot, and on the chariot, they placed a dummy figure pointing toward the south.

Then he told all the armies fighting in battle that, as long as they were confused by thick fog, they could immediately tell which direction was east, south, west, or north by looking at the dummy figure on the south-pointing chariot.

After the compass appeared in the Western Han dynasty, records of the south-pointing chariot appeared in many later dynasties. These records show that the south-pointing chariot was used throughout history, and was only used in grand ceremonies. It was a necessary part of the emperor's ceremonial procession. The "Nan Qi" book also records an interesting incident: during the Liu Song dynasty of the Northern and Southern Dynasties, after conquering Guanzhong, the emperor obtained a compass as a war prize—but it had only the outer shell and no internal parts. So, when the emperor traveled, people were hidden inside the chariot to operate it. This shows clearly that the presence of a south-pointing chariot was important for the emperor's dignity and prestige.
