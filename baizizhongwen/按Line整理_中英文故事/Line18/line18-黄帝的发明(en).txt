The Yellow Emperor's Inventions

The most famous figure of the Yellow Emperor's time was Cangjie, known as the "Historian Emperor." Some people say he was a minister of the Yellow Emperor, others claim he was an ancient ruler before the Yellow Emperor. Some say Cangjie and the Historian Emperor were one and the same, while others insist that Cangjie invented writing and the Historian Emperor created pictures—two completely different people. Indeed, there are many conflicting accounts.

However, the earliest forms of writing were actually pictures, so it makes sense to consider Cangjie and the Historian Emperor as one person. As for whether he ruled before the Yellow Emperor or lived at the same time as him and served as a minister, since these are all part of myth and legend, there's no need to investigate further. Let us speak of this "Historian Emperor" Cangjie. He was born with something extraordinary. It is said that he had a wide dragon-like face with four eyes glowing with light. Even as an infant, he loved to pick up a brush and scribble here and there—his drawings seemed interesting and not at all annoying. As he grew older, he began to think deeply, exploring the changes and patterns of the world and the universe.

He lifted his head to observe the curved patterns of the Quixing, lowered his head to examine the patterns on the turtle's back, the feathers of birds and the winding landscapes of mountains and rivers. Based on these natural phenomena, he would constantly point and draw on his palm, and thus invented writing.

It is said that when Cangjie's extraordinary invention first appeared, the sky was startled and began to pour down "millet rain," and ghosts were frightened and cried out in fear at night. Why? Because they feared people would abandon farming and instead pursue the small gain of carving characters with needles and knives, leading to future hunger. So, a little millet was sent down to relieve future famine—a warning to humanity. As for the ghosts, they were afraid of being accused by these terrifying characters, so they cried out at night. This shows that the invention of writing was indeed a momentous event in human cultural history, shaking heaven and earth and moving even the spirits.

The Yellow Emperor ordered Ling Lun to create the system of musical scales. There is a story, almost mythical, that Ling Lun traveled from the west of Daxia all the way to the north of Kunlun Mountain.

In the valley of Xie Xi, Cangjie gathered bamboo. He selected straight, hollow bamboo with even thickness, cut it between the two nodes, making each piece three inches and nine tenths long, and blew into it to establish the pitch of the Yellow Bell (Huangzhong) tuning. Then, following this ratio, he made twelve bamboo tubes and took them down to Kunlun Mountain to listen to the sound of the phoenix. By comparing the phoenix's call with the Yellow Bell tuning, he found that the differences in pitch perfectly matched the twelve different tunings. Thus, based on the Yellow Bell tuning and the phoenix's call, he adjusted the lengths of the twelve bamboo tubes to create twelve distinct musical tones. This was how Ling Lun established the system of musical tuning.

The Yellow Emperor then ordered Ling Lun and Rong Jiang to cast twelve bronze bells, to match the five fundamental tones—gong, shang, jiao, zhi, and yu—and use them in the performance of the grand musical pieces, "Liuying" and "Jiushao."

Especially on the day of the second month, the day of Yi Mao, when the sun appeared in the direction of the Kai star, the Yellow Emperor officially began to perform a magnificent and grand musical piece using the twelve bronze bells he had cast. He named this piece "Xianchi." "Xianchi" originally referred to a constellation, meaning "heavenly pool," and now it was used as the name of the Yellow Emperor's musical composition. It was probably not just a musical performance, but also included dance movements, already showing the early form of drama. During the Warring States period, Zhao Jianzi lay ill for seven days and seven nights and dreamed of "Juan Tian Guang Le," which is likely a development from the heavenly music of the Yellow Emperor's "Xianchi." The overall conductor of the heavenly music was undoubtedly Ling Lun, who later became associated with the immortal Hongya. In Zhang Heng's "Western Capital Ode," it is described that during a lively performance of fish and dragon acrobatics in the Western Capital, Chang'an, there was a figure dressed in delicate feathers and fur, named Hongya, standing among the musicians and directing the performance.

So later generations called all performers, including musicians and directors, "ling guan," starting from the time of the Yellow Emperor when Ling Lun established the rules of music and performed musical pieces.

The Yellow Emperor also ordered Yin Shou to make a mirror. There is a myth about this. It is said that when the Yellow Emperor met the Western Queen Mother at Wangwu Mountain, he ordered Yin Shou to cast twelve large mirrors to reflect the grand scenes of banquets and feasts. Each mirror was used according to the month of the year, with different specially made mirrors for each month. Another legend says that the Yellow Emperor made fifteen smaller mirrors. The first mirror had a diameter of one foot five inches, "following the number of a full moon," and each subsequent mirror decreased in size, so the last one had a diameter of just one inch. In the early Tang Dynasty, Wang Du obtained the eighth of these fifteen mirrors from a descendant of Hou Sheng from the Sui Dynasty. This mirror had a diameter of eight inches, and on its back were finely carved patterns of tortoise, dragon, phoenix, tiger, the eight trigrams, and the twelve earthly branches, as well as ancient characters. When Wang Du obtained this mirror, he encountered many strange people and strange events, and he used it to see many ghosts and demons.

It is said that he wrote a story based on his own experiences, called "The Record of the Ancient Mirror," which later became the foundation of Tang dynasty novellas.

After Wu Peng invented medicine, during the Yellow Emperor's time, there were three famous doctors. The first was Yu Fu. It is said that his medical skills were extremely advanced, and he mastered complete surgical techniques. He treated patients without using herbal medicines, needles, massage, or heat applications. Instead, he could directly examine the original condition of the five internal organs and six viscera, cut open the skin, dissect muscles, tie off tendons and ligaments, squeeze out diseased bone marrow and brain marrow, separate the fatty and oily substances above and below the diaphragm, gently peel away the opaque membranes with his fingers, and thoroughly clean out the intestines and other internal organs such as the heart, liver, spleen, lungs, and kidneys. After undergoing this surgical treatment, the patient's mind and body would completely recover and transform. Therefore, later legends say that Yu Fu's medical skill was so powerful that it could make a coffin carrying a corpse move backward.
