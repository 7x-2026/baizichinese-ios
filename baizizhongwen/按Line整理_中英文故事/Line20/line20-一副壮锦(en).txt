A Piece of Zhuang Brocade

Long ago, by the foot of a big mountain, there was a flat piece of land. On this flat land, there were several thatched huts. Living in one of them was a woman named Dabu. Her husband had died, and she was left with three children. The eldest was named Lemo, the second Leduie, and the youngest Lere.

Dabu was a master at weaving Zhuang brocade. The flowers, birds, and animals woven into her brocade looked so lively and real. People all bought her brocade to make baby belts, quilt covers, and bed rugs. The whole family lived day by day on the skill of Dabu's hands.

One day, Dabu took several pieces of brocade to the market to sell. She saw a colorful painting in a shop. The painting was very beautiful. It showed tall houses, lovely gardens, vast fields, fruit orchards, vegetable patches, and fish ponds, with flocks of cows, sheep, chickens, and ducks. She looked at it again and again, and her heart filled with joy. Originally, she had planned to use all the money from selling her brocade to buy rice, but because she loved the painting, she bought a little less rice and took the painting home instead.

On her way home, Dabu sat several times by the roadside, opening the painting to look at it. She whispered to herself, "It would be wonderful if I could live in such a village."

When she got home, she opened the picture and showed it to her sons, and all the boys laughed happily.

Dabu said to her big son, "Lemo, we'd better live in a village like this!"

Lemo pushed his lips aside and said, "Mom, that's just a dream!"

Dabu said to her second son, "Leduie, we'd be better off living in a village like this!"

Leduie also pushed his lips aside and said, "Mom, that's for the next life!"

Dabu frowned and said to her little one, "Lere, we can't live in such a village. I'll go mad with boredom." She sighed deeply.

Lere thought for a moment and comforted his mother, "Mom, your embroidery is so beautiful—the things on the fabric look so lively. Why don't you embroider this picture onto the fabric? As you look at it, you'll feel like you're living right in this beautiful village."

Dabu thought for a while and said, "You're right. I'll do it that way. Otherwise, I really will go mad with boredom."

Dabu bought five-colored silk threads, adjusted her loom, and began to embroider according to the picture.

Weaved day after day, month after month.

Lemo and Leduie were very unhappy with Mom doing this. They often came over and pulled her hands, saying, "Mom, you keep weaving without selling anything. We have to rely on you cutting firewood to earn rice—how hard it is for us!"

Lere said to his elder brothers, "Let Mom weave. If Mom doesn't weave, she'll go mad. You think cutting firewood is hard? I'll go alone and cut all the firewood."

So the family's life depended entirely on Lere climbing the mountains day and night to cut firewood.

Dabu never stopped weaving. At night, she lit pine resin to light up the room, and the smoke was so strong it burned her eyes, turning them red and sore. Still, Dabu wouldn't stop. After one year, Dabu's tears fell onto the silk, and on those tears, she wove a clear little river and a round fish pond. After two years, her eye blood dripped onto the silk, and on that blood, she wove a bright red sun and vibrant flowers.

Weaving and weaving, it took three years before this piece of Zhuang brocade was finally completed.

This piece of Zhuang brocade is so beautiful! Several tall houses, blue roofs, green walls, red columns, and a yellow gate. In front of the houses is a large garden with bright flowers. There's a fish pond in the garden, and goldfish are swimming and waving their tails. To the left of the houses is an orchard, with fruit trees full of red fruit and various birds flying around the trees. To the right is a vegetable garden, filled with green vegetables and yellow melons and fruits. Behind the houses is a wide meadow, with cow and sheep pens and chicken and duck coops. Cows and sheep are eating grass on the meadow, while chickens and ducks are pecking at insects. Near the houses, at the foot of the mountain, there's a large field full of golden corn and rice. A clear river flows past the village, and a bright red sun shines from the sky.

"Wow, wow, this Zhuang brocade is so beautiful!" the three children exclaimed.

Dabu stretched her back, rubbed her red eye shadow, and opened her mouth to laugh—laughing so freely and happily.

Suddenly, a strong wind blew from the west, "crack!" and it rolled the piece of Zhuang brocade out of the door and into the sky, flying straight toward the east.

Dabu rushed out after it, waving her hands wildly and shouting overhead. "Oh no! In a flash, the Zhuang brocade is gone!"

Dabu fainted at the gate. The three brothers carried their mother back, laid her on the bed, and poured her a bowl of ginger soup. Slowly, Dabu came to her senses. She said to her eldest son, "Lemo, go east and bring back the Zhuang brocade. It's the very life of Dabu!"

Lemo nodded, put on his grass shoes, and headed east. After more than a month, he reached the mountain pass.

At the mountain pass stood a stone house. To the right of the house was a large stone horse. The stone horse opened its mouth, wanting to eat a basket of red hawberries nearby. Sitting at the door was an old woman with white hair. When she saw Lemo approaching, she asked, "Child, where are you going?"  

Lemo said, "I'm looking for a piece of Zhuang brocade. My mother wove it over three years, and a strong wind blew it eastward."

Grandma said: "The Zhuang brocade comes from a group of fairy women on the Eastern Sun Mountain. They saw your mother's skill in weaving and wanted to take it as a model. To reach them is no easy task! First, you must have two of your teeth knocked out and put into the mouth of my big stone horse. Only then will the stone horse have teeth and be able to move and eat the persimmons by its side. After it eats ten persimmons, you can climb onto its back, and it will carry you to Sun Mountain. On the way, you must pass through a volcano filled with roaring fire. The stone horse will go through the flames, and you must hold tightly to your teeth and endure it—never cry out. If you scream even once, you'll turn into ash and burn up. After crossing the volcano, you'll reach the vast ocean. The waves in the sea are fierce and will hurl ice blocks at you. You must hold tightly to your teeth and stay calm—never shiver. If you even flinch, the waves will sink you beneath the sea. Once you cross the ocean, you'll arrive at Sun Mountain, where you can ask the fairies to return your mother's Zhuang brocade."

Lemo touched his teeth and thought about being burned by fire, and about the waves crashing against him. His face turned instantly pale.

Grandma looked at his face and smiled, saying, "Child, you can't bear such hardships. Don't go! I'll give you a small box of gold. Go back home and live a peaceful life."

Grandma took out a small iron box of gold from the stone house and gave it to Lemo. Lemo took the small box and turned around and walked away.

On the way home, Lemo kept thinking, "With just this small box of gold, my life will be better. But I can't take the lesson home—four people sharing it isn't as comfortable as one person enjoying it all." Thinking like this, he decided not to go home. Instead, he turned and headed toward a big city.

Dabu was thin from illness and lay in bed, waiting for two months without seeing Lemo return. She said to her second son, "Leduie, go east and find the Zhuang brocade. That brocade is the very life of Dabu!"

Leduie nodded and put on grass shoes, heading east. After walking for a month, he reached the mountain pass and met Grandma sitting at the door of the stone house. Grandma said to him the same words again.

Leduie rubbed his teeth, thinking of the fire burning his body and the waves crashing against him. His face turned suddenly pale.

The old grandmother gave him a small iron box of gold. Holding the small iron box, he, like his older brother, refused to go home and set off toward the big city.

Dabu lay sick in bed, waiting for another two months. Her body had shrunk thin, like a dry stick. Every day she looked out the window and cried. Her red eyes had wept so much that they had turned blind—she could no longer see anything.

One day, Lere said to his mother, "Mama! Brothers one and two haven't come back. I think they must have met some trouble on the way. I'll go. I must find the Zhuang brocade back!"

Dabu thought for a moment and said, "Lere, go! Be careful of your health on the journey. The neighbors near by will take care of me."

Lere put on grass shoes, straightened his chest, and walked steadily eastward. In just half a month, he reached the mountain pass. There, he met the old grandmother sitting by the stone house door.

The old woman said to him the same thing again and added, "Child, your elder brother and second brother each took a small box of gold back. You should take one too!"

Lere said, "No, I want to go get back the Zhuang Brocade!" Then he picked up a stone, knocked out his two teeth, and placed them in the mouth of the big stone horse. The stone horse moved, opened its mouth, and began eating the hawberries. When it had eaten ten, Lere immediately jumped onto the horse's back, grabbed the mane, and pulled his legs tight. The stone horse lifted its head and let out a long cry, "Bilipu lu," and rushed off toward the east.

After three days and three nights, they reached the Volcano of Emission. Red flames rushed toward people and horses, burning the skin and making a hissing sound. Lere stayed on the horse's back, biting down hard on his teeth to endure the pain. After about half a day, he crossed the volcano and jumped into the vast ocean. The waves, carrying big ice blocks, crashed against him, making him feel cold and painful. Lere stayed on the horse's back, biting down hard on his teeth to endure the pain. After half a day, they reached the other side—there it was, the Sun Mountain. The sun warmed gently on Lere's body, and it felt so comfortable!

On the top of the sun mountain stood a magnificent house, glowing with golden light, from which came the singing and laughter of women.

Lere pressed his legs together, and the stone horse neighed, leaping into the air and instantly arriving at the door of the big house. Lere dismounted and stepped inside, where he saw a large group of beautiful fairies gathering in the hall weaving brocade. Dabu's Zhuang brocade was placed in the center, and everyone learned to weave by copying it.

When they saw Lere enter, they were startled. Lere explained his purpose. One of the fairies said, "Very well, we can finish weaving tonight, and give it to you tomorrow morning. Please wait here overnight."

Lere agreed. The fairies gave him many magical fruits to eat, and the taste was absolutely delicious!

Lere felt very tired and leaned against a chair, falling asleep instantly.

At night, the fairies hung a night-light pearl in the hall, illuminating the room with bright light. They began weaving all night long.

One of the fairies, dressed in red, was the most skillful with her hands and feet—she finished weaving alone first.

She compared the fabric she had woven with Dabu's, and thought Dabu's was much better: the sun was bright red, the fish pond clear and shiny, the flowers fresh and tender, and the cows and sheep lively and full of spirit.

The red fairy whispered to herself, "If I could live in this Zhuang brocade, it would be wonderful." Seeing that others had not yet finished weaving, she casually picked up a thread and embroidered her own image on Dabu's Zhuang brocade: standing by the fish pond, gazing at the bright red flowers.

Lere awoke and it was already deep night. The fairies had all returned to their rooms to sleep. Under the bright glow of the pearls, he saw Dabu's Zhuang brocade still lying on the table. He thought, "What if they don't give me the brocade tomorrow? Dabu has been sick in bed for a long time—there's no more time to delay! I must take the brocade and leave right away."

Lere stood up, picked up Dabu's Zhuang brocade, folded it neatly, and tucked it into the pocket of his inner robe. He stepped out of the door, mounted the horse, and with a quick twist of his legs, the stone horse surged forward under the moonlight, "Bilipu lu" — racing off into the night.

Lere bit down on his teeth, riding on the horse and crossing the vast ocean, climbing over the angry mountains, and soon returned to the mountain pass.

The old woman stood before the stone house, smiling warmly and said, "Child, get off the horse!"

Lere jumped down from the horse. The old woman pulled out a tooth from the horse's mouth and placed it into Lere's mouth. The stone horse then stood still by the hawthorn tree.

The old woman took out a pair of deer leather shoes from the stone house and gave them to Lere, saying, "Child, put on the deer leather shoes and hurry back home—Dabu is about to die!"

Lere put on the deer leather shoes, kicked his legs twice, and in a flash arrived at home. He saw Dabu lying on the bed, thin as a piece of dry wood, breathing weakly and humming faintly—she was truly about to pass away.

Lere walked to the bed, called out, "Dabu!" Then he took out the Zhuang brocade from his chest and spread it before her. The bright light instantly lit up Dabu's eyes. She jumped up quickly, smiling warmly and looking at the Zhuang brocade she had woven herself for three years. She said, "Child, our hut is dark and dim inside—let's take it outside under the sunlight so we can see it properly."

The two girls walked out to the door and laid out the Zhuang brocade on the ground. A gentle breeze blew in, and the brocade slowly unfolded, stretching out until it covered the entire plain, stretching for several miles.

The thatched hut where Dabu used to live was gone. In its place stood several grand, dazzling houses, surrounded by gardens, fruit orchards, vegetable plots, fields, and herds of cattle and sheep—exactly as beautifully woven into the brocade. Dabu and Lere stood in front of the big houses.

Suddenly, Dabu saw a red-dressed girl sitting by the fish pond in the garden, watching the flowers. Dabu hurried over and asked her. The girl said she was a fairy, brought here because she looked just like a pattern embroidered on the Zhuang brocade.

Dabu invited the fairy to live in the house and stayed with her. Lere married the beautiful girl and lived a happy life.

Dabu also invited poor people from nearby to move into this village, because when she was sick, they had cared for her.

One day, two beggars came from beside the village. They were Lemo and Leduie.

They got the old woman's gold and went to the city to eat and drink wildly. Soon the gold was gone, and they had to become beggars, asking for alms to survive.

They arrived at this beautiful village and saw Dabu and Lere singing happily in the garden. Remembering their past, they felt ashamed and couldn't face them. So they picked up their begging sticks and ran away.
