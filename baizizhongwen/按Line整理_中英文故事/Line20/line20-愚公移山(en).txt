The Foolish Old Man Moves the Mountains

The Taihang and Wangwu mountains, each spanning seven hundred li in area and reaching seven or eight thousand zhang in height, originally stood south of the Ji Zhou region and north of the Yellow River.

There lived an old man named Yu Gong in the north of the mountains, nearly ninety years old, who lived facing the mountains. He was troubled by the blocked roads on the northern side of the mountains, which made travel long and difficult. So he gathered his whole family and said, "I will work with you all to level these steep mountains, so that we can reach the southern part of Yuzhou and the southern bank of the Han River. Is that possible?" Everyone quickly agreed with him. His wife raised a question: "With your strength, you can't even reduce a small mountain like Kuipu—how could you possibly move the great Taihang and Wangwu mountains? And where would you put all the earth and stones you dig up?" Everyone replied, "We should carry them all to the edge of the Bohai Sea, beyond Yinsu." So the old man led his three sons and grandsons, chiseling stone and digging earth, using baskets to carry the soil and stones to the edge of the Bohai Sea. A young orphan, only about seven or eight years old, from a widow in the nearby village of Jingcheng, also jumped and ran to help them. Through the cold winters and hot summers, through the changing of seasons, they made one round trip.

The wise old man from Hequ laughed and discouraged Yu Gong, saying, "You are too foolish! With only a few years left in your life and such little strength, you can't even remove a single blade of grass from the mountain. How could you possibly move the soil and stones?"  
Yu Gong sighed deeply and said, "You are stubborn and closed-minded—so stubborn that you can't even understand the truth. Even a child or an orphan would be wiser than you. Even if I die, my son will still be alive; my son will have a grandson, and my grandson will have a son; that son will have a son, and that son will have a grandson. From generation to generation, there is no end. But the mountain will never grow taller or bigger—what is there to worry about if we keep digging?"  
The wise old man from Hequ had no words to reply.

When the mountain god heard about Yu Gong's effort to move the mountains, he feared that Yu Gong would keep digging forever, so he reported the matter to the Heavenly Emperor. Moved by Yu Gong's sincere heart, the Heavenly Emperor ordered two sons of the Great God Kua'eo to carry away the two great mountains—one placed in the eastern part of Shuofang, and the other in the southern part of Yongzhou.

From then on, there were no high mountains blocking the southern part of Jizhou all the way down to the south of the Han River.
