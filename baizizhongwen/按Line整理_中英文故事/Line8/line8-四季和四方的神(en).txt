The Gods of the Four Seasons and Directions

Long, long ago, the sun rose in the east and set in the west, creating the four seasons and the four directions. In ancient China, each season and direction was ruled by a different god. The god of the east was Fuxi, who had a human face and a dragon body. His assistant was the Wood God, Jumang, who had a human face and a bird body, wearing a green robe. He held a round compass in his hand and rode a dragon, ruling over spring.

The god of the south was Yandi, who had a human face and a beast body. His assistant was the Fire God, Zhurong, who had a human face and a beast body, wearing a red robe. He held a balance rod in his hand and rode two dragons, ruling over summer.

The god of the west was Shaohao, who had a human face and a tiger body. His assistant was his son, the Metal God, Rushou, who had a human face and tiger paws, covered in white fur, with a snake hanging from his left ear. He held a curved ruler in his hand and rode two dragons, ruling over winter.

The god of the north is named Zhuanxu. He has a human face and a beast body. His assistant is the water god Xuanming, who has a human face and a bird body, covered in black fur, with two green snakes hanging from his ears. He holds a weighing hammer in his hand. Xuanming walks on two serpents and governs winter.

The god of the center is named the Yellow Emperor. His appearance is very unusual—he has four faces and lives in the center of the heavenly court. His assistant is the earth god Houtu, who holds a rope and helps the Yellow Emperor govern the four seasons and the four directions.
